# The look

Everything should look like it came off one of the cards: chunky shapes, brown
marker outlines, colored pencil on white paper. It's 3D, but it never looks
glossy or realistic.

## Pets

Each pet is built from its square drawing, as a whole blocky character. Every square
becomes a block, so the **front of the pet's head is the drawing**, exactly as on the card.
Behind the face the head is a deep block with its back edges rounded off; the ears are
thin blocks set back on top, and the body and legs are smaller blocks under the middle of
the head, with a short tail standing up behind. The eyes, nose and cheeks are painted
only on the front. Underneath they're the color around them.

What wraps round the back comes from the card: Buddy's head is black behind the tan face
and Choco's dark purple behind the light one, Mango's brown and orange patches carry on
over the back of the head, Halsy's goggles have a strap all the way round, Plufulous's
glasses reach back along the sides under a rainbow crest, Scarlet Ivy's hair hangs down
the back, Zeus's blue ears flop out sideways, and Fluff Puff has a round puff of a tail.

The pet sits in its kart facing forward, so during a race you see the back of its head.
Karts are small beside the road, so the course reads big around them.
The pick screen is where you see faces.

## Karts and the world

Karts are simple shapes that read as the food: a taco shell full of lettuce, a bowl of
ramen with chopsticks as a spoiler.

Each track is a place drawn from a pet's point of view:

The pets are tiny in the kitchen: the counter's things are many times their size.

A **ramp** is a narrow wedge, a third of the road wide: its slope is a boost pad (orange,
white arrows pointing ahead), and its low sides and the nose that kicks up at the top are
in the track's colors (brown on the Backyard, red and white in the Kitchen, blue and white
on Snow, pink and cream in the Bakery, blue and yellow in the Toy Room). Taking off throws
orange and yellow puffs, like a boost. A **hazard** fills a branch from border to border: long grass with tufts,
or water with white ripples (blue, or syrup-brown in the Bakery; yellow wheat or brown mud on the long tracks). A sinking kart drops
below the surface in a spray of puffs. Where the road **forks**, both roads have the
track's border along their outer edges and down the island between them, and the curbs
stop where one road opens into the other.

The **verge** is a flat band past the curbs in a color between the road and the ground
(gravel in the Park, longer grass in the Backyard, slush on Snow, sugar in the Bakery,
felt in the Toy Room, wet sand on the Beach, straw on the Farm, undergrowth in the
Jungle), widening and narrowing gradually. The **border** follows where the karts are
stopped, so it stands out across the open ground in most places and swings in close
on the outside of sharp bends; scenery stays outside it.

On the long tracks the ground, scenery and hills spread out to match, and the camera over
the pick screen rises higher and sees further, with the fade to paper pushed back to suit.

A track's **picture** on the pick screen is drawn, not rendered: the roads from above with
brown outlines and the curb color, in the track's road colors, on little squares of its
ground, with hazards in their colors, jumps as orange dots and a checkered start line.

Where a big hill in the distance reaches over the road (on the Snow, Backyard, Park and
long tracks), the road goes through it in a **tunnel**: a grey stone arch with a stone portal
at each end, and no border inside. Hills and tunnels never stand across another road.

## The drawn style

- **Marker outlines** in the cards' brown (`#5a3214`) go around every shape and between
  every color change, which is how the drawings outline each square of color. They're
  wobbly, and they redraw 8 times a second, so they "boil" like hand-drawn animation.
- **Colored pencil shading**: each color has a lit tone and two darker tones. The darker
  tones are hatched with pencil lines instead of smoothly shaded.
- **Paper**: pencil grain everywhere, a blue pencil-scribble sky, and distance fades to
  white paper instead of fog. Far-off hills look like an unfinished sketch.
